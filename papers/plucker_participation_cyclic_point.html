Reader guide

Plücker participation measures how evenly a point of the Grassmannian spreads its weight across its Plücker coordinates. On the positive top cell the paper gives a proof candidate that participation is at most an explicit product of squared cotangents, and that the regular cyclic point is the only point attaining it. The argument rewrites the problem as the comass of a single form, diagonalizes the sign kernel by a finite Fourier transform, and applies the Wirtinger bound for powers of a two-form. The boundary cells are the subject of a companion paper.

Abstract

We present a proof candidate, pending external mathematical review, for the maximum over the positive top cell \(\Gr_{>0}(k,n)\) of the Plücker participation ratio \[\mathsf{Part}(C)= \frac{\left(\sum_{|I|=k}\Delta_I(C)\right)^2} {\sum_{|I|=k}\Delta_I(C)^2}.\] The maximum is attained at the regular cyclic point and is \[\begin{cases} \displaystyle \prod_{r=1}^{m}\cot^2\!\left(\frac{(2r-1)\pi}{2n}\right), & k=2m,\\[1.2em] \displaystyle n\prod_{r=1}^{m}\cot^2\!\left(\frac{r\pi}{n}\right), & k=2m+1. \end{cases}\] Moreover the regular cyclic point is the unique global maximizer (for each \((k,n)\) the equality locus is a single cyclic Fourier plane). The proof converts the positive Plücker participation problem into the comass of the all-ones Plücker \(k\)-form. That form is \(\Omega^m/m!\) in even degree and \(\eta\wedge\Omega^m/m!\) in odd degree, where \(\Omega=\sum_{i<j} e_i\wedge e_j\) is the sign-kernel two-form and \(\eta=\sum_i e_i\). A finite Fourier diagonalization of the sign kernel, followed by the standard Wirtinger comass bound for powers of a two-form, gives the displayed product. The equality planes are the cyclic Fourier planes, whose Plücker coordinates are the regular cyclic chord products.
