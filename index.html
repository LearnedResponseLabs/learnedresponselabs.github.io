Galashin’s Conjecture 4.3, via a convex gradient

Galashin’s measurement map turns angles on a circle into a point of a positroid cell’s critical variety. The paper gives a proof candidate that, for every loopless cell, this map is a homeomorphism: in the right coordinates it is the gradient of an explicit convex function, so the angles are read back by a convex minimization.

Rf ∘ Measf = Gf = ∇Ψf,   Ψf(θ) = Σe L(δe(θ)),   L′(u) = log sin u

The argument in three steps

  1. Angles make a point

    Galashin’s map Measf sends admissible angles on a gauge slice to the positive critical cell. His Conjecture 4.3 says it is a homeomorphism.

  2. The point is a gradient

    After the Muller–Speyer twist and in logarithmic necklace coordinates, the map becomes Gf = ∇Ψf, an exact identity resting on the row differences of Galashin’s exponent matrix.

  3. Convexity reads it back

    The Hessian B diag(cot δ) BT is positive on the slice because the crossing edges split into winding-one cycles. So Gf is injective, and its inverse is a convex minimization.
