From a chamber to a positive matrix

This explorer draws the cyclic hyperplane arrangement behind the m=1 amplituhedron. Karp and Williams, The m=1 amplituhedron and cyclic hyperplane arrangements (arXiv:1608.08288), Theorem 6.16, show that the amplituhedron is homeomorphic to the arrangement's bounded complex. The explorer's tiles are the bounded top-dimensional chambers. It illustrates and computes; its finite checks are not proofs.

1. Start with the sign changes

Each hyperplane evaluates the polynomial p(t)=1+x₁t+⋯+xₖtᵏ at a chosen positive parameter tᵢ. An interior point has a sign vector σ. In the indexing used here, σ starts with + and changes sign at exactly k gaps among 1,…,n−1.

At k=2,n=5, the first tile has σ=+−−−+ and gaps {1,4}. There are C(4,2)=6 indexed chambers. This is the default view.

2. Distinguish geometry from projection

At k=1 the actual geometry consists of intervals between the points −1/tᵢ. At k=2 it consists of bounded polygons cut out by the lines 1+tᵢx₁+tᵢ²x₂=0.

For k≥3 the explorer shows a two-dimensional SVD shadow and reports the captured vertex-cloud variance. A large percentage does not make the shadow an embedding or preserve all geometric features: distinct chambers can overlap. Use the facet adjacency graph, not visible overlaps, to determine which tiles are neighbors.

The optional standardized SVD equalizes coordinate variances first; it is a different projection with a separately labelled variance statistic. The x₁,x₂ view is a coordinate projection.

3. Read the positroid blocks

The explorer groups consecutive sign-change gaps into runs. A run of length L occupies L+1 consecutive columns and contributes a top cell of Gr(L,L+1). Columns outside all such windows are loops. The direct sum has dimension k.

For {1,4}, the two rank-one blocks occupy {1,2} and {4,5}, while column 3 is a loop. The associated permutation is (2,1,3,5,4). The block rule follows Karp and Williams, Lemma 4.2 and Corollary 5.14. The code is in py/core.py.

4. Follow a strand

The plabic panel draws each strand as a cubic Bézier path. Top numbers name legs; the colored bottom number names the starting strand at its exit. Click a curve or choose a start → destination pair to isolate it. The trip, face-label, and bridge-alpha displays are independent.

Face labels come from a simplified recursion and pass support-membership checks for every preset. That is a finite check, and it does not certify the recursion in general. The display makes no claim to convert its bridge parameters α into the column weights below.

5. Move a point without changing the tile

A length-L block uses the L×(L+1) Vandermonde matrix on local nodes 1,…,L+1, multiplied by positive column weights. The direct sum gives C. Form Y=CZ with Zᵢ=(1,tᵢ,…,tᵢᵏ), compute the signed cofactor vector of Y, and normalize its constant coordinate to one. The remaining coordinates give x.

All selected rational weights, matrix minors, cofactors, and chamber-sign checks use exact fractions. In the default tile, unit weights give x=(−12/13,2/13). Changing w₁ to 3/2 gives x=(−183/188,31/188). The black diamond moves accordingly.

A useful nonuniqueness example: multiply all column weights of one block by the same positive factor. This rescales its rows, leaving the row span and the image x unchanged. By contrast, changing just one participating column weight generally moves x. A loop-column weight has no effect.

6. Inspect exact vertices

Vertices come from an explicit exact construction. At the intersection of hyperplanes indexed by I, with |I|=k, the polynomial is ∏ᵢ∈ᴵ(1−t/tᵢ). Its coefficients supply the vertex coordinates. Signed inequalities determine incidence, and common-facet rank determines adjacency.

As a check, all 44 presets were compared with an earlier floating-point half-space solver of the same arrangements: 1,898 tiles, with the same order, vertex counts and 3,725 facet edges, and vertex coordinates agreeing to within 2.3×10⁻¹¹.

Scope

These are canonical moment-curve arrangements with positive strictly ordered tᵢ. The app does not implement colliding hyperplanes, arbitrary external Z, a universal face-label proof, a canonical-form calculation, or a bridge-weight-to-column-weight map.
