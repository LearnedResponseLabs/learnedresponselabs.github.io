Two graphs, two kinds of move. A chord flip changes the tile; a quiver mutation keeps its chord diagram fixed.
Tiles, seeds and the two graphs

A tile is a region obtained by mapping a standard BCFW cell into the amplituhedron. Here a chord diagram indexes a tile. The left graph joins diagrams that differ by one chord; this is the flip used in this explorer.

A seed specifies variables and a quiver of arrows encoding their exchange relations. A mutation replaces one mutable variable by the sum of its two exchange monomials divided by that variable, and changes the arrows. Frozen variables cannot mutate. The quiver here includes domino variables, with the sticky same-end identity β of the child = α of its immediate containing chord drawn once.

The right graph follows mutations while keeping the tile fixed. It tracks an auxiliary principal-coefficient C-matrix and groups states by its unordered rows. Finite completed searches are compared with the type’s cluster count. The display stops at the selected cap; no hidden counting continues. A stopped search says nothing about finiteness.

See the source, Definitions 10.4 and 10.8, Remark 10.5 and Lemma 10.13, and the reading guide. Numerical exploration and interface checks are not mathematical proofs.
